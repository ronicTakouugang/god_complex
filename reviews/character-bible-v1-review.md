# Editorial review: Character Bible v1

Reviewer stance: acquisitions editor at a premium graphic-novel imprint. The question is not whether this sounds cinematic. It's whether it would survive 30 issues, an artist, a showrunner, and a reader who guesses ahead.

---

## 1. Originality

**Distinctive**
- **The cost mechanism:** the world forgets the harm, and a person somewhere holds the forgetting. This is a real idea. I haven't seen it done.
- **The unholdable likeness:** a hero with no mask because no face sticks. It gives her a secret identity for structural reasons, not costume reasons.
- **The cultural spine:** a hero from a culture of *saudade* whose power erases memory. It's specific, and it carries meaning.
- **Survivors who keep their wounds by choice** (partial undoing): "she left me my scars."

**Derivative**
- **Amnesiac hero searching for her past.** This is one of the most worn templates in genre fiction. The bible leans on it hard.
- **Dying-grandmother origin.** It's affecting, but it's the default emotional trigger.
- **The regulator antagonist** (a registration act, a government that wants to use the hero). This is familiar from decades of superhero event comics.
- **City-scale disaster climax** (the earthquake). This is spectacle as a substitute for a decision.
- **Blind ally who "knows her truly."** It's close to the mystic-blind-seer trope, and his job stack (piano tuner, pirate DJ, scanner listener) reads as quirks.

## 2. Psychology

She is mostly believable. "I am what I do" is a good engine for someone with no past. **But her psychology is a list of traits** (gratitude-craving, dissociation, can't refuse children, guilt converted to action) **rather than one engine that generates scenes.**

The fear that "Celeste was a bad person" is planted and **never paid off**: Celeste turns out to be simply good. A fear that turns out to be groundless is wasted.

## 3. Power system

This is **the weakest area.** It is overloaded and has three real contradictions:

1. **L4 is labelled FACT ("nearest in meaning"), and then Revelation 2 overturns it** ("nearest to her"). A FACT the story later reverses is a BELIEF that was mislabelled. An artist or writer working from v1 would write the wrong rule.
2. **Solvang's Protocol needs costs to be steerable, but no one knows they are until Revelation 2.** Her central plan makes no sense at the point she introduces it.
3. **"Small saves, small costs" in 2031 contradicts L1/L3.** Saving a choking child saves a life, and a life costs a relationship-level memory. Mara's loss (five years of her son) proves this, in the same paragraph that calls those saves small.

On top of that, **there are too many mechanics:** the seven-minute window, sight or touch, acute-only, physical-only, consent, L1–L7, the Pull, the Heft, likeness, residue-reading, self-undoing, the cold, exhaustion, bleaching, the seam, the irises, the blink. **Residue-reading is a plot convenience**: a detective tool added to solve the story's problem. The sufficiency clock (L7) is bookkeeping, not drama. The seven-minute "locked" explanation stops working once the backstory changes.

## 4. Moral conflict

**Strong in principle and weak in scenes.** Before Revelation 2, Lua doesn't know who pays, so every decision is the same binary: save or don't. Readers will feel the arithmetic but not a choice. The finale offers **five options**, which spreads out the tension. A great moral choice has two or three options, and every one costs the chooser personally.

## 5. Antagonist

Solvang does challenge Lua's worldview (fairness and consent against improvised mercy), which is the right instinct. But:
- She has **no advantage over Lua.** She opposes her, but she doesn't know anything Lua doesn't.
- "She has volunteered four times" makes her **a second version of the protagonist** rather than a counterweight.
- The Unsaved, the *Amnésticos*, memory insurance and the grey market crowd the field. **Too many opposing forces, none of them developed.**

## 6. Emotional stakes

**The payers are statistics:** 1,140 cases, 900 villagers. Only Mara and her son Tobi have faces. Collective memory loss is conceptually strong but hard to *feel* without one person to carry it. Readers will care about Lua. As written, they won't care as much about the people she empties, and the whole dilemma depends on that.

## 7. Long-term storytelling

- **Revelation 1** (the dreams are the payers' memories) is good and lands with a specific face.
- **Revelation 2** (the cost isn't random) is good, and it's the backbone of the series.
- **Revelation 3** ("she was the first payer") **is guessable from page one.** The origin shows her losing her memory at the moment of the first save, and any attentive reader connects the two immediately. It's a setup presented as a twist.
- The sufficiency clock gives the endgame a mechanical deadline, not an emotional one.

## 8. Visual potential

**This one fails as written.** A grey cardigan and a lanyard don't make a silhouette. The face rule ("a soft white area where others have line work"; a "white bloom" in photos) creates **a serious problem: the face of a Black Cape Verdean woman rendered as a white blank.** No artist should be handed that brief. Too many visual tells (seam, irises, bleach, white-out) compete, and there's no single emblem a designer can build a system on.

---

## The five weakest aspects

| # | Weakness | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | **The power system is overloaded and contradicts itself** (L4 vs Revelation 2, the Protocol before steering exists, "small" saves) | Each department would build a different version of the rules |
| **2** | **Revelation 3 is guessable, and her past is too clean** | The final twist doesn't surprise anyone, and the "was I bad?" fear is wasted |
| **3** | **The payers have no faces** | The dilemma is only as strong as the reader's care for the people who pay |
| **4** | **The finale is spread out and driven by spectacle**, with a mechanical clock | The climax becomes a menu, not a choice |
| **5** | **Weak silhouette, and a face rule with a racial-rendering problem** | The character can't be recognised, and the brief is unsafe to give an artist |

## Improvements adopted in v2

1. **Replace L4 and L7 with a single rule: the cost falls on whatever she holds closest at the moment she says yes.** Without her knowing, that's her longing. Deliberately, it can be anyone she pictures. This one rule:
   - explains the pattern, which looks random, rhymes with what she longs for, and converges on her heritage;
   - makes Solvang's Protocol work: show Lua the volunteer's photograph;
   - creates the emotional clock, because **the more she loves someone, the closer the cost comes to them.**

   It also lets me cut residue-reading (the dreams do that job), the sufficiency bookkeeping, the cold, the seam and the irises.
2. **Rebuild Revelation 3: Night Zero was not the beginning.** Celeste had the power for five months, and dementia patients paid, where forgetting goes unnoticed. She worked it out. On Night Zero she **chose** to pay with herself, partly as atonement and partly to escape knowing what she'd done. She recorded a confession in her own voice, which is the one thing about her that survives. This pays off the fear, isn't guessable, and makes the origin her choice rather than an accident.
3. **Give the payers one face that recurs: Djina Lopes**, the village schoolteacher in Ribeira da Prata. Her mother is the singing woman in Lua's dreams. Mara and Tobi remain the second face. Lua can't return Djina's memories, but she can **teach the songs again**. That is the story's only legitimate grace, and it doesn't break the irreversibility rule.
4. **Narrow the finale to three options, each of which costs Lua:** let the default happen (her people forget), hold herself (lose everyone she has become), or refuse (the city dies). Solvang's volunteers are the temptation that falls short.
5. **Build a real silhouette** (knee-length carer's cardigan, radio headphones around the neck, cropped head, the carer's kneel) and **one emblem, the blank badge (a white rectangle)**. **Replace the face rule:** her face is hidden by staging in drawn panels and appears as a colour-preserving motion smear in in-world photos. It is never a white blank.

Also tightened: Solvang **knows the steering rule before Lua does**, because she ran an experiment on herself. That gives her an advantage over Lua and a single, decisive mirror moment. Duarte is reduced to one coherent job: a former radio host who repairs radios and listens to the emergency scanner. The civilian movements become background texture.
