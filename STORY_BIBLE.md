# THE GOD COMPLEX — Story Bible

> Canon for the project. Every page, line of copy, and motion decision should trace back to this file.
> Items marked **[PROPOSED]** are working choices. They can be changed, but update this file when they are.
> **The hero, cast, power rules and world are defined in full in [CHARACTER_BIBLE.md](CHARACTER_BIBLE.md), which takes precedence over this file.**

---

## 1. Logline

A mysterious individual possesses extraordinary powers. Every time they save someone, another person somewhere in the world loses a memory. The hero must decide whether saving humanity is worth destroying the memories that make it human.

## 2. The three pillars

| Pillar | What it gives us | Rule for the site |
|---|---|---|
| **Power with a cost** | Every miracle is paid for by someone else | Never show a rescue without its cost close by |
| **Moral conflict** | There is no villain to punch and no status quo to restore | Never frame the hero as purely good or purely bad |
| **Mystery** | The visitor works out the link between hero, losses, and origin | Reveal things in order. Don't give the answer on the first screen |

## 3. The hero

- **Alias:** **AMNESTY**, coined by the press after the Tagus Miracle. Detractors call her *A Ladra*, "the Thief."
- **Government designation:** **Subject LETHE**, after the river of forgetting.
- **Identity:** In truth, Celeste Semedo. She calls herself Lua and doesn't know who she was. See CHARACTER_BIBLE §1.
- **In-world documents:** until Revelation 3, investigators can't establish the subject's sex, age or appearance, because witnesses can't hold her likeness. Case files use *the subject* or *they/them*. Writers' canon and Lua's own scenes use *she/her*.
- **Design consequence:** The hero is never shown as a clear portrait. She appears as a pale blur, a gap in a photo, a redaction, or a figure described by contradictory witness statements.

## 4. The power system — "The Ledger"

The rules in full are in CHARACTER_BIBLE §3.2. In short:

1. **Every undoing has a price.** It removes memory of equal weight. A life costs a relationship. A mass rescue costs a community its shared history.
2. **It looks random.** That's what the public and the hero believe.
3. **It is not random (hidden until Revelation 2).** The cost falls on whatever the hero holds closest in the instant she says yes. Usually that's unconscious: her longing for what she lost. The more she loves someone, the closer the cost comes to them.
4. **Memories do not come back.** Nothing in this universe can restore them, and the story never cheats on this. Something forgotten can only be learned again, as new.

The abilities themselves (strength, speed, matter control) are kept vague on purpose. What matters is the cost, not the moves.

## 5. The mystery — escalation

| Phase | What the world sees | What investigators find |
|---|---|---|
| I. **The Miracle** | Rescues. Gratitude. A legend forms | Nothing yet |
| II. **The Static** | Scattered cases of people forgetting small things: names, birthdays, a route home | A neurologist notices clusters of a very specific kind of memory loss |
| III. **The Pattern** | People forget their families. Whole towns lose their collective history | The losses line up with the rescues in time and in emotional weight |
| IV. **The Ledger** | Public panic. "Stop saving us" vs. "Save us anyway" | Every rescue is matched to a victim |
| V. **The Origin** | — | The truth (see §6) |
| VI. **The Choice** | — | The hero has to decide, and so does the visitor |

## 6. The origin (central twist, now canon)

**The first person to forget was the hero.**

But Night Zero wasn't the beginning. For five months beforehand, Celeste Semedo had been undoing harm in a dementia home, and residents paid, where nobody notices forgetting. She worked it out. On Night Zero (14 March 2031) she saved her dying grandmother and **chose** to hold herself, paying with every memory of her own life. It was partly atonement and partly escape. Her confession, in her own voice, waits in a missing-person evidence box. Full detail: CHARACTER_BIBLE §4 and §8.

No one can hold her likeness because a face needs an identity to attach to. As she accepts the name Celeste, her likeness begins to hold.

The "God Complex" has two meanings:
- the hero deciding whose life is worth whose memory;
- the investigators and governments wanting to *use* the hero, deciding who gets saved and who pays.

## 7. Key characters

See CHARACTER_BIBLE §6 for full profiles.

- **Duarte Mbala**, the ally who believes in her. A blind piano tuner, and the only stranger who can recognise her (by voice).
- **Dr Mara Okafor**, the one who distrusts her. A neurologist and a payer herself, who publishes the Okafor Correlation. She is the visitor's guide through the evidence.
- **Dr Ingrid Solvang**, the antagonist. Director of the **Continuity Office**, which runs Case LETHE. Its documents are the site's main texture.
- **Ivone Semedo**, her grandmother. The only person who remembers who Lua was.
- **Survivors and Payers**, the two populations: those who were saved, and those who paid. Some people are both.
- **The Archivists** (who record everything on paper) and **the Unsaved** (who wear DO NOT SAVE tags), the two civilian movements.

## 8. Tone and art direction

**The site should feel like** a premium graphic novel, a classified government investigation, and a cinematic universe at the same time.

**Textures:** case files, redaction bars, evidence photos with gaps, typewritten and stamped documents, witness transcripts, maps with plotted incidents, inked comic panels.

**It must avoid:**
- the standard superhero landing page (dramatic portrait, bio, power cards);
- triumphant hero imagery without the cost alongside it;
- references or visual echoes of existing franchises. This is an original property.

**The site's core experiential idea [PROPOSED]:** *the site itself forgets.* As the visitor moves deeper, earlier content fades, degrades, or gets redacted. Learning the truth costs the visitor something, just as every rescue costs the world something.

## 9. Canon rules (quick reference)

1. Every rescue is shown with its cost.
2. The hero's face is hidden by staging, never by white-out, until Revelation 3 (CHARACTER_BIBLE §9.3).
3. Lost memories never return.
4. In-world case files refer to the hero as *the subject* or *they/them* until Revelation 3. The writers' voice uses *she/her*.
5. The origin is revealed late and never on the first screen.
6. No borrowing from existing superhero franchises.
