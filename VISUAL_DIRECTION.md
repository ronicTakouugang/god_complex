## 0. Design problem

Five constraints from canon shape every option:

1. **She has no mask and no costume,** because no one can hold her likeness, so she doesn't need either.
2. **Her face is hidden by staging, never by erasure.** White never covers her face (bible §9.3).
3. **The power works by subtraction:** colour drains, sound stops, the harm reverses. There are no beams, auras or glowing lines.
4. **She is a carer, not a fighter.** Her signature action is kneeling, not striking.
5. **She must still be recognisable in solid black.**

So the question is: *how do you make an iconic superhero out of someone who wears work clothes and whose face you can't show?* The answer has to come from **shape, posture, hands, and one emblem.**

## 1. Three directions

### Direction A: **NIGHT SHIFT** (grounded carer realism)

**Concept.** She wears what she wore on Night Zero and never took off: the Santa Luzia night-shift uniform, worn soft by two years of living in it. The heroic image is the opposite of armour, because care itself is the costume.

- **Silhouette:** a long column (knee-length cardigan), a loop at the collar (headphones around the neck), a small cropped head, and the kneel.
- **Costume:** grey-blue knee-length knit cardigan, charcoal tee, navy work trousers worn pale at the knees, non-slip care shoes, a lanyard with the **blank badge**.
- **Face:** hidden by staging. Her hands carry the emotion instead.
- **Palette:** a quiet grey-blue figure in a warm, saturated Lisbon.

| Strengths | Weaknesses |
|---|---|
| Matches canon exactly. Every element has a story reason. | The cardigan is a soft shape. At small sizes it could read as "any woman in a coat." |
| Humane, grounded, completely unlike any superhero costume. Fits the premium graphic-novel register. | Risks looking drab if the world around her isn't richly coloured. |
| The kneel and the hands give artists something more expressive than a face. | Needs strict silhouette discipline (§2.1) to stay iconic. |
| Easy to translate into photography, illustration and web imagery. | |

### Direction B: **THE LEDGER COAT** (an accumulated garment)

**Concept.** Lua builds a coat. She starts with Duarte's old radio-station overcoat, and after every rescue she sews in a token from that place: a strip of a survivor's scarf, a ferry-ticket stub, a button. Inside the lining she writes the names of *the ones she left*. The coat **grows heavier with each rescue.** It's the Ledger made visible.

- **Silhouette:** wide, asymmetric and heavy. Ankle-length, with a lopsided weight of patches on one shoulder.
- **Costume:** a dense patchwork collage over a dark wool base, with ink-written lining visible when the coat swings open.
- **Face:** shadowed by a high, stiff collar.
- **Palette:** a multicoloured patchwork figure against a grey institutional world.

| Strengths | Weaknesses |
|---|---|
| The design changes over time, literally: the coat at issue 1 and issue 30 are different objects. | **The patchwork cloak is a familiar heroic image.** It drifts toward fantasy "wanderer" costumes. |
| Very rich for merchandise and close-up storytelling. | It turns her into a **performer** who builds a costume, which contradicts her humility and her "I'm not anyone" psychology. |
| Lining full of names is a strong reveal moment. | It's **visually busy**. Detail fights the subtraction language, and it's hard to animate cleanly on the web. |
| | The high collar becomes a mask by another name, an easy way out of the face rule. |

### Direction C: **LONG EXPOSURE** (photographic ghost)

**Concept.** Her identity is the trace she leaves on images. She's always shown the way cameras see her: **multiple exposure, motion trails, a figure in several positions at once.** The clothes are minimal (black work clothes). What she *is* visually is the smear, plus one object, the blank badge, which is the only sharp element in every image.

- **Silhouette:** a stack of overlapping semi-transparent silhouettes. The sharp white badge rectangle is the anchor.
- **Costume:** deliberately generic black. The image technique is the costume.
- **Face:** always within the blur, which is canon for in-world photos.
- **Palette:** a chromatic smear of her own colours through the motion trail.

| Strengths | Weaknesses |
|---|---|
| Perfectly expresses the unholdable likeness. The most original image of the three. | **It fails the silhouette test:** a smear isn't a shape. Fill it with solid black and nothing is left. |
| Very web-native and photographic, and striking on a screen. | It reads as an **effect, not a character.** Readers attach to the technique, not to her. |
| The single sharp badge in a blurred image is a powerful graphic idea. | Comic panels need a body to act. A permanent smear can't kneel, hold a hand, or grieve. |
| | Hard to evolve. After Revelation 3 the whole look has to be replaced. |

## 2. Recommendation: **Direction A, NIGHT SHIFT**, taking one rule from each of the others

A is the only direction where **every visual element is also a story element**, and the only one that doesn't fight the character's psychology. Its weakness (a soft silhouette) can be fixed with design discipline. B's and C's weaknesses are built into their concepts.

**Two rules adopted from the other directions, nothing more:**
- **From B, accumulation as mending, not collecting.** She **darns** the cardigan as it wears through. Each patch of darning is in yarn from the place where it tore. The garment slowly records her history, quietly. It's a carer's repair, not a costume being built.
- **From C, the long exposure for in-world photography only.** Every photo or video of her in the story world shows the ghosted, colour-keeping motion smear, with the **blank badge as the one sharp element.** This is how the case files and the website's evidence imagery depict her. In drawn panels she stays fully embodied.

### 2.1 The recognisable silhouette

Every image of her must keep these five shape features, which read at thumbnail size in solid black:

1. **The column.** A knee-length cardigan falling straight from narrow shoulders, slightly A-tapered. It is never belted.
2. **The collar loop.** Over-ear headphones resting around her neck. The cups stick out past the line of her shoulders in profile and front view. *(Removed at a key story point, §6.)*
3. **The small round head.** Close-cropped natural hair, so the head's outline is clean. **Never a hood.**
4. **The drop.** The lanyard falls to the sternum and ends in a hard rectangle, which shows whenever the cardigan hangs open.
5. **The kneel.** One knee down, the other foot planted, one hand flat on the injured person or the ground, head bowed below shoulder height. It reads as **a low triangle anchored by an open hand.** She never stands over anyone she saves.

**Silhouette test:** a solid-black thumbnail of her in the kneel must be distinguishable from any other kneeling figure in the comic. The loop and the column are what make it so.

### 2.2 Proportions and physical presence

- **1.68 m, lean.** Realistic proportions, about **7.25 heads tall**. Never heroic 8–9 head proportions.
- **Narrow, sloping shoulders.** Long arms. **Large, capable hands**, deliberately drawn a little bigger than average. **The hands do the work a face would**, carrying the emotion in close-ups.
- **A carer's carriage:** head slightly forward, leaning toward people, weight low, very little wasted movement.
- **Presence:** standing, she takes up little space and disappears in a crowd (which the story needs). Kneeling, she **anchors the composition.** Panels and layouts should put her low in the frame, with the world rising above her.
- **Skin:** deep brown, with warm undertones. Light it with care: **warm key light, never desaturated**, including during an undoing (§4).

The hem has stretched unevenly. **Darned patches** in mismatched yarn: Tagus blue, Ribeira ochre, Duarte's brown. **Left pocket:** her notebook. **Right pocket:** the scanner. |

**Colour rules**

- Saturation belongs to the world. **The story's visual tension is warm world against quiet figure.**

### 2.5 The face, and what replaces it

- **In drawn panels:** her face is hidden by **staging** only: cropping at the jaw, a turned head, back-lighting, shadow, a broken reflection, a foreground object. Her skin, neck, ears and hands are drawn fully.
- **In in-world media:** the long exposure, a ghosted smear that keeps her colours, with **the badge as the one sharp element.**
- **Identity-defining features in place of a mask:** **the hands** (big, ink-marked, open), **the badge** (blank), **the collar loop**, and **the kneel.**
- **Never:** a white blob, blur or bleach over her face. Never a hood, mask, veil, or high collar used as a mask.

### 2.6 Symbols and recurring motifs

Each one has a story reason. Without one, it doesn't appear.

| Motif | Meaning | Where it appears |
|---|---|---|
| **The blank badge** (white rectangle) | An identity with nothing written on it. **The series emblem.** | Her chest, the cover logo, the basic shape of the site's redaction system |
| **The hand inscription** | A self that has to be rewritten every night | Close-ups, chapter openings, handwritten interface elements |
| **The open flat hand** | Mercy, and contact as the trigger for her power | The kneel, the cover, the logo variant |
| **The collar loop** (headphones) | Duarte, the voice, being guided | Silhouette, until the betrayal |
| **The notebooks** (*Os que eu deixei*) | The dead she refused, and the people who paid | Interiors, evidence imagery |
| **The red door** | Someone else's home, mistaken for hers | Ribeira da Prata, Revelation 1 |
| **The paper-grey ring** | The reach of an undoing | Power scenes, transitions |
| **The blink frame** | A memory leaving someone | Single-panel beats, the site's transition between chapters |
| **Stamp Red marks** | Institutional ownership | Case files, the Continuity Office's documents |
| **DO NOT SAVE tags** | Refusal, from the public's side | World texture |

**Rejected motifs** (no story reason): glowing veins, energy halos, clock or hourglass imagery for the seven-minute window (too literal), wings, crosses or religious haloes, fractured glass, circuit lines.

## 3. Ordinary life vs. undoing

| | **Ordinary** | **Undoing** |
|---|---|---|
| **Posture** | Slightly hunched, hands in pockets, headphones on, eyes down. She deliberately keeps herself small. | **The kneel.** Totally still, one hand flat, head bowed. |
| **Her colour** | Quiet, close to the background | **She is the only thing in full colour.** For two seconds, the quietest figure becomes the most vivid thing in the world. |
| **Surroundings** | Warm, busy, saturated Lisbon | Drained to paper grey, outward from her |
| **Sound** (cinematic and web) | Ambient city sound | **Complete silence** |
| **Camera and framing** | Background figure. She is often not the focus of her own panels. | Low angle, centred, with the world arranged around her |
| **Hands** | Hidden in her sleeves or pockets | Bare, open, flat |

## 4. How the power changes the environment

**The sequence (canon, from bible §3.4):**
1. **The Pull** (before): the sound of the scene thins. Visually, the background loses detail first, as if focus were narrowing.
2. **The yes:** a **paper-grey ring** spreads from her open hand across everything she can perceive. The grey has the fine grain of old paper. **Her own colour stays at full strength.**
3. **Reversal:** inside the grey, the harm runs backwards *physically*. Blood draws back, beams return to place, water empties off the ferry deck. It's rendered with soft directional motion blur, like a film run in reverse, **never frozen time**: rain keeps falling throughout.
4. **Return:** colour floods back from the **edges of the frame inward** over about two seconds. The last thing to regain its colour is the ground under her hand.
5. **Afterwards:** survivors in colour, uninjured or deliberately left their scars. Lua is often already standing and turning away.

**At the payer's end:** **one blink-white frame.** Then the panel or shot repeats exactly, and *nothing visible has changed*. The horror is in the expression, not the environment. **Artist rule:** draw a payer's before and after panels as identical compositions, with only the face changed.

**Scale:** a small undoing is a grey ring a few metres wide. The Tagus Miracle is a **river-wide** field of grey seen from the bank: the largest image in the series.

## 5. Three poses that show her psychology

1. **THE KNEEL** (mercy and compulsion). One knee down, one hand flat on the body, the other arm out for balance, head bowed. The cardigan pools on the ground around her. *Shows: she lowers herself to save someone. It's care, and it's the only place she feels whole.*
2. **THE REFUSAL** (holding back). Standing, fists closed at her sides, **body tilted backwards as if leaning against wind**, chin up, fixed on someone dying out of frame. One foot set behind her. *Shows: refusing takes physical effort. Saying no is harder than saying yes.*
3. **THE HAND** (identity). Seated on the edge of a narrow bed, back to the viewer, cardigan around her shoulders, headphones on, looking at the inscription on the back of her left hand under a small lamp. *Shows: a self that has to be rewritten every night. It's the most private image of her.*

## 6. How the design evolves

Each stage ties to the knowledge table in bible §8.1. Changes to her **silhouette** are reserved for the story's turning points.

| Stage | Period | Changes | Silhouette |
|---|---|---|---|
| **0. Celeste** *(flashback, only at Revelation 3)* | 2027–Mar 2031 | Crisp new uniform, long braided hair, **badge filled: CELESTE S.** in printed type. **Face visible.** Warm palette. | No loop, longer head shape |
| **1. Desconhecida → Lua** | 2031 | Same cardigan, now stained. Hospital-cropped hair. **Blank badge.** First hand inscription. | Column, small head |
| **2. AMNESTY** | 2031–Feb 2033 | **Headphones added** (Duarte's scanner). First darned patches. Notebooks in her pockets. | **Full iconic silhouette: column, loop, head, kneel** |

| **4. After the betrayal** | late 2033 | **Headphones removed.** The voice has been used against her. | **The loop is gone.** The silhouette is visibly incomplete, and readers should feel the absence. |
| **5. After Revelation 3** | early 2034 | Ivone returns **the name card** she tore off on Night Zero and kept for three years. Lua carries it in her pocket, **not yet in the badge holder.** Her face starts to appear at moments of chosen identity. | Column, head, kneel |
| **6. The Lisbon Choice** (three designed end states) | 2034 | **(1) Longing chooses:** the card goes into the holder, *CELESTE*, but the mornas are gone. **(2) She holds herself:** the badge is blank again, she is a stranger to everyone, and her face is permanently unseen. **(3) She refuses:** she wears the card, kneeling in a grey city that never regains its colour. | Each ends with the kneel, for a different reason |

## 7. Art-direction brief

### 7.1 For concept artists and image generation

**Project:** THE GOD COMPLEX, an original graphic-novel universe. **Register:** a premium literary graphic novel combined with a classified investigation file. It is not a superhero blockbuster.

**The hero:** Lua (AMNESTY), a 35-year-old Black Cape Verdean woman, 1.68 m, lean, realistic proportions, large capable hands, close-cropped natural hair. She wears a worn knee-length grey-blue knit cardigan (shawl collar, deep pockets, darned patches), a charcoal tee, navy work trousers pale at the knees, black care clogs, worn over-ear headphones around her neck, and a teal lanyard with an **empty badge holder**. She has blue ballpoint writing on the back of her left hand.

**Visual style:** inked line work with a confident brush and restrained flat colour. A warm, saturated Lisbon world (ochre, azulejo blue, terracotta, Atlantic teal) against her quiet grey-blue. Cinematic composition, with her low in the frame. Paper grain throughout.

**Hard rules:**
1. **Her face is never visible.** Hide it with cropping, a turned head, back-lighting or shadow. **Never** use white-out, blur, mask, hood or veil on her face.
2. **Her skin is warm and never desaturated,** even when the world around her has turned grey.
3. **The power is subtractive:** a grey ring spreading outward, harm reversing, silence. No glow, beams, particles, sparks or auras.
4. **She kneels; she doesn't fight.**
5. **No superhero costume elements:** no capes, armour, emblems on the chest, tech, or spandex.

**Negative prompt / exclusion list:** cape, cloak, hood, mask, armour, spandex, glowing eyes, glowing hands, energy aura, particles, lightning, halo, chest emblem, clear frontal face, white blur on face, superhero pose, hands on hips, fists raised, flying, futuristic tech, neon, lens flare.

### 7.2 Prompts for exploring the three directions

Run each **4–8 times**. Judge them with the solid-black silhouette test (§7.4) before looking at colour.

**A. NIGHT SHIFT**
> Full-body character design sheet, a 35-year-old Black Cape Verdean woman, lean, realistic proportions, close-cropped natural hair, worn knee-length grey-blue knit cardigan with shawl collar and darned patches, charcoal tee, navy work trousers pale at the knees, black care clogs, old over-ear studio headphones resting around her neck, teal lanyard with an empty clear badge holder. Three views: front, side, back. Face turned away or cropped by the frame in every view. Inked graphic-novel line work, restrained flat colour, paper grain, neutral background. Also a small solid-black silhouette of each view.

**B. THE LEDGER COAT**
> Full-body character design sheet, a 35-year-old Black Cape Verdean woman, close-cropped hair, heavy ankle-length dark wool overcoat covered in sewn-on fragments (scarf strips, ticket stubs, buttons), asymmetric weight of patches on one shoulder, handwritten names visible on the inner lining, high stiff collar, work boots. Face hidden in shadow, never masked. Three views. Inked graphic-novel style, flat colour, paper grain. Solid-black silhouettes included.

**C. LONG EXPOSURE**
> Photographic long-exposure image of a woman in plain black work clothes walking through a Lisbon street at night: multiple ghosted overlapping positions of her body, motion trails that keep her deep brown skin tone and clothing colours, one sharp element only, an empty white badge holder on a teal lanyard. Face lost in the motion, not whited out. 35mm film grain, warm streetlight, azulejo-tiled walls.

### 7.3 Prompts for refining the chosen direction (A)

1. **Turnaround:** prompt A above, plus *"close-up of the left hand with blue ballpoint text 'Eu sou a Lua' on the back of the hand; close-up of the cracked headphone cup taped with silver tape; close-up of the darned cardigan elbow."*
2. **The Kneel:** *"Graphic-novel panel, the woman in the grey-blue cardigan kneeling on wet cobblestones, one knee down, one hand flat on the chest of an injured cyclist, head bowed so her face is hidden, cardigan pooled on the ground, low camera, Lisbon street at night, warm saturated colour."*
3. **The Undoing:** same scene, *"a ring of paper-grey desaturation spreading outward from her hand across the street and buildings; she alone remains in full warm colour; the bicycle wreckage reversing with soft motion blur; rain still falling; no glow, no particles."*
4. **The Refusal:** *"standing figure in a long grey-blue cardigan seen from behind and slightly to the side, fists closed, body leaning backward as if into strong wind, headphones around her neck, an ambulance's lights out of focus in the distance, her face turned away."*
5. **The Hand:** *"small room at night, the woman seated on the edge of a narrow bed, back to the viewer, cardigan around her shoulders, headphones on, looking at writing on the back of her left hand under a small desk lamp; notebooks stacked; warm lamplight, deep shadow."*
6. **The Tagus Miracle:** *"wide cinematic panel from the Lisbon riverbank at dusk, a tiny kneeling figure at the water's edge, the whole Tagus river and a ferry drained to paper-grey, the ferry rising back upright, the city behind still in warm colour, storm clouds."*
7. **Emblem still life** (safe hero imagery for the site): *"overhead object photograph on a grey-blue wool cardigan: an empty badge holder on a teal lanyard printed 'CASA DE REPOUSO SANTA LUZIA', a worn notebook, old studio headphones with a cracked cup taped in silver, a blue ballpoint pen; soft daylight, archival documentation style."*

### 7.4 Selection protocol (do this before any website work)

1. **The silhouette test.** Fill each candidate solid black at 64 px tall. Can you recognise her (the column, the loop, the head) against five other characters? If not, reject it.
2. **The face rule.** Reject anything with a visible face, or white or blur over her face.
3. **The narrative test.** For every visible element, name its story reason (§2.6). Anything without one gets cut.
4. **The skin test.** In undoing scenes, check that her skin keeps its full warmth.
5. **Lock a single turnaround and three pose sheets** as the reference. All later images, site imagery included, derive from those, not from individual early explorations.

### 7.5 Guidance for the website's imagery

- **Primary imagery is evidence, not portraits:** Continuity Office photographs (with the long-exposure smear and the sharp badge), objects (badge, notebooks, headphones, the red door), places (Cais do Sodré, Santa Luzia, the Ribeira terraces), and documents with Stamp Red marks.
- **Comic panels** come in at story beats (the Tagus Miracle, the fountain, the Refusal) as inked flat-colour work.
- **The site never opens on a hero portrait.** The strongest opening image is the empty badge, or the kneel in silhouette.
- **The site's visual grammar comes from the power:** colour draining to paper grey, silence, the blink frame between chapters, white rectangles as the redaction shape. **White rectangles are the world's forgetting.** They redact documents and places, **never her face.**
- **Use the colour roles as defined:** world warm, Lua quiet, the Office in Stamp Red, the self in Ballpoint Blue.
- **The reveal order of imagery follows the knowledge table in bible §8.1.** Celeste's face and the filled badge appear only in the Revelation 3 chapter.
