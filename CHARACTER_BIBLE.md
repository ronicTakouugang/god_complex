## 0. The hero in one paragraph

**Lua** doesn't know her name is **Celeste Semedo**. She can make physical harm unhappen. Each time she does, someone forgets something of equal weight. She has no past, no papers, and a face no one can hold in mind. The world calls her **AMNESTY**. She believes the cost falls at random. In fact it falls on **whatever she holds closest in the instant she says yes**. That has meant strangers whose memories echo her own longing, then her own people, and soon the people she loves. She has done this before, and she chose to forget it.

### Why the culture matters (FACT)

Cape Verdean *morna* is the music of **saudade**: longing for what's absent, carried across an ocean by a people defined by emigration. The hero comes from a culture whose central art form is remembering, and she becomes what makes the world forget. This drives Revelation 2. It must be portrayed specifically and with dignity (real Kriolu, real musical forms, ordinary home life), never as exotic decoration. Places that suffer harm in the story (Ribeira da Prata, Casa Santa Luzia) are fictional.

## 2. Psychology

### The engine (one sentence)

**Saving people is the only self Lua has, and every person she lets herself love becomes the next person in the cost's path.** Each arc turns on that collision between the need to act and the danger of attachment.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Core desire** | To be *someone*. With no past, she feels real only in the act: *"I am what I do."* Under that is a smaller wish, to be remembered by one person. |
| **Active goal (2032–33)** | To find where she came from. She follows her dreams of a red door, a valley and a singing woman. |
| **Greatest fear** | That there's no one inside her, just the power. And that Celeste was someone worse. *(The second fear is justified: Revelation 3.)* |
| **Moral code** | *"Ninguém morre à minha frente."* ("No one dies in front of me.") She answers only harm she witnesses directly, and never goes looking for it. The story breaks this rule step by step: the scanner, the Protocol, the city. |
| **Relationship with power** | Relief, close to addiction. The moment of undoing is the only time nothing in her is missing. Afterwards there's guilt, and a pull to do it again. |

### Contradictions

- **She treasures memory and destroys it.** She keeps notebooks and a shoebox of strangers' objects, and she is history's largest destroyer of memory.
- **She longs to be remembered and cannot be held.**
- **She claims no control and is choosing.** *(Revelation 2.)*
- **She wants to belong and is dangerous to belong to.** The closer someone comes, the more exposed they are.
- **She is herself the argument, both ways.** She is proof that a person with no memories is still fully a person, and proof of how much that person has lost.

### Weaknesses (and the scenes they produce)

- **She can't refuse a child.** Every refusal she has ever made was an adult.
- **She attaches instantly to anyone who can recognise her.** That makes her loyal to Duarte to the point of blindness, which sets up the betrayal.
- **She turns guilt into action.** A new save feels like paying off an old debt.

### What makes her human

She hums mornas she can't name, perfectly in tune. Her night-shift habits are intact: blankets, water, a hand on the shoulder. Her humour is dry. She introduces herself to the same baker every morning, and it has become a joke between them. Each night she writes *"Eu sou a Lua"* ("I am Lua") on the back of her left hand.

## 3. Powers

### 3.1 Primary: **Undoing** (FACT)

Lua can make **physical harm unhappen.** A wound closes as if it never opened. A collapsed beam is back in place. A capsized ferry is upright and its passengers are dry on deck. **It is not time travel.** Everyone present remembers that it happened. The world forgets the wound; the people don't.

> **The idea in one line:** she doesn't heal. **She makes the world forget a harm, and that forgetting has to be held by someone's mind.**

| Limit | Rule |
|---|---|
| **Window** | Only harm from the **last seven minutes**. After that it is permanent. |
| **Perception** | She must **see or touch** the harm. Its scale is limited only by what she can perceive. |
| **Kind** | **Acute physical harm** only: injury, accident, violence, an acute medical event such as a cardiac arrest, a stroke, or drowning. She can't undo disease, ageing, grief, or anyone's memory loss, including the losses her own power causes. |
| **Consent** | It **never** acts without her yes. The yes can take a fraction of a second. |
| **Partial undoing** | She can undo *part* of a harm, for example the death but not the broken leg, and the cost drops accordingly. Her survivors often keep their injuries. *"She left me my scars."* |
| **Herself** | She can undo harm to her own body. She is very hard to kill, but every time she survives, someone pays. |
| **Toll on her** | After a large undoing she sleeps for 12 hours or more. Otherwise she is physically ordinary: no strength, flight, or armour. |

### 3.2 The Ledger: rules of the cost

The name comes from Mara Okafor's notebooks and has become the public term.

| # | Rule | Status |
|---|---|---|
| **L1** | **Equal weight.** Each undoing removes memory of **equal human weight** to the harm undone. Weight means meaning, not mass. A broken window costs a trivial memory. **A life costs a relationship** (a person, a marriage, a childhood). **A mass undoing costs shared memory**, which only communities hold, so whole communities forget their collective history. | FACT |
| **L2** | **Personal memory only.** Only episodic, autobiographical memory is taken: events, people, shared history. Skills, language and general knowledge remain. | FACT |
| **L3** | **Never returns.** Lost memories never come back. No therapy, technology or power restores them. (Something lost can be *learned again from scratch*, like a song taught to someone who forgot it, but it comes back as new, not as remembered.) | FACT. Absolute. |
| **L4** | **The cost falls on whatever she holds closest in the instant she says yes.** Usually that's unconscious, and it's her **longing** for her missing childhood, grandmother, home, sea and songs. So payers are strangers whose memories echo what she misses, and over time the pattern converges on the Cape Verdean diaspora. If she holds **a specific person** in mind, that person pays. If the closest thing is **herself**, she pays. | **HIDDEN FACT** (Revelation 2) |
| **L4-B** | *"The cost falls at random, somewhere in the world."* | **BELIEF**, held by Lua, the public, and Mara's early model |

**What follows from L4 (FACT):**
- **Small costs often land on Lua herself,** because she is closest to herself. She is constantly losing small things: a meal, a conversation, where she left her keys. That's why she keeps notebooks.
- **Loving someone puts them in the path.** As she lets Duarte, Mara and Ivone in, they become candidates. This is the story's emotional clock.
- **The cost can be steered deliberately.** That is what Solvang's Protocol exploits (§6.3).

### 3.3 Secondary traits (FACT, deliberately few)

| Trait | Description |
|---|---|
| **The Pull** | She senses **irreversible harm about to happen** nearby. She experiences it as sound draining away, as if underwater. **Its range grows with every use**: a few metres in 2030, several streets by 2033. |
| **The Heft** | Before saying yes, she feels **how heavy the cost will be**, as weight, like lifting something blind. She can feel the size, but not who will pay. |
| **Unholdable likeness** | Since Night Zero, **no one can retain her appearance.** In the moment people see her normally. Within minutes their memory of her face, build, height and apparent age slips away, and witness accounts contradict each other. On camera, she appears as a **ghosted motion smear that keeps her colours** (never a white blank; see §9). **Her voice is unaffected**, and recordings of it are clear. That makes her voice both her one human tether and the one way to track her. |
| **The dreams** | She dreams fragments of **some payers' memories**. *(She believes they are her own past; see Revelation 1.)* |

### 3.4 What it looks and sounds like (FACT)

- **Undoing:** colour drains outward from Lua to a **paper-grey** across everything she perceives. **All sound cuts.** The harm reverses. Colour floods back from the edges in about two seconds.
- **At the payer's end, "the blink":** half a second of white silence and the smell of hot dust, like an old projector bulb. The payer feels nothing missing afterwards. Others notice.

### 3.5 When she refuses (FACT)

**Nothing supernatural happens.** There's no backlash and no build-up. **People die.** She feels them through the Pull and remembers each one. She keeps their names in a separate notebook: *Os que eu deixei*, "the ones I left." Refusing costs her nothing except the people who die. That is the dilemma.

### 3.6 Control, prediction, reversal

| | |
|---|---|
| Whether it happens | **Yes:** consent |
| How much | **Yes:** partial undoing |
| Who pays | **BELIEF:** no. **HIDDEN FACT:** yes, through what she holds closest (L4). |
| Predicting the size | **Yes:** the Heft |
| Predicting who pays | Only by knowing what she was holding in mind |
| Reversing | **Never** (L3) |

## 4. Origin

### 4.1 The hidden first chapter (Oct 2030 – Mar 2031) *(HIDDEN FACT, Revelation 3)*

**October 2030.** Albano Reis, 84, a resident, falls down a stairwell at Santa Luzia and breaks his neck. Celeste kneels beside him and refuses to let it happen, and **it unhappens**. **This is the first undoing.** In the same instant, another resident, Glória Pais, forgets her late husband completely. Staff note it as "progression."

**Over five months there are about a dozen undoings: falls, choking, a stroke.** Each time, a resident Celeste cares for (whoever she's holding closest) loses a whole relationship. In a dementia ward, nobody notices the forgetting. Celeste does. In her Night Archive recordings, she slowly works it out, and then she keeps doing it anyway for a while, because the alternative is watching people die.

**14 March 2031, 01:30.** She records a confession on her phone: what she can do, what it costs, who has paid. She records a decision too: *"Next time, it's me."*

**03:12.** Ivone has a cardiac arrest. Resuscitation fails. Celeste holds her grandmother and holds **herself** closest, and says:

> *"Não me esqueças."* ("Don't forget me.")

Ivone's heart restarts. **Celeste's entire autobiographical past is erased.** It is partly atonement and partly escape: she pays the debt, and she stops knowing what she did. She keeps her languages, her care skills, and the songs. At 03:40 a camera records a ghosted figure leaving by the front door. Her phone stays in her staff locker. It ends up boxed as evidence in a missing-person file and is not listened to for three years.

### 4.2 What Lua believes (BELIEF)

That she lost her memory in some unknown trauma, and that her powers began afterwards. That Night Zero, which she can't remember, was the start.

### 4.3 The first person she saves as Lua (14 May 2031, 17:42)

She undoes a tram accident on Rua da Conceição and saves **Duarte Mbala** (§6.1). He's the first person who can **recognise her again**, because he knows her by voice. He gives her his spare room.

**Through 2031–32** the public hears of a rescue roughly every month: a choking child, a cyclist, a fall from scaffolding. **The real number is far higher.** Between May 2031 and January 2033 she undoes 228 harms that cost a single stranger each, plus the Tagus. Only 31 of them are ever reported, because no witness can describe her and most happen at night on streets where nobody is filming. (FACT, fixed by the archive's interim analysis, AEL-IA-07.) **Each life costs a stranger a relationship**, and each stranger's loss echoes what Lua longs for: childhoods, grandmothers, homes by the sea. The cases are scattered worldwide, so doctors call them strokes or early dementia. **October 2031:** a neurologist in Geneva, **Mara Okafor**, loses every memory of her son's first five years (§6.2).

**Named payers (FACT, from the archive):**
- **Duarte's tram rescue** was paid for by a woman in Porto, who forgot her grandmother (AEL-0117).
- **On 2 September 2031, 08:52,** a boy was pulled from under a bus in Alcântara. At the same minute, **I. M.**, 38, an accountant in Setúbal, forgot her seven-year-old son **Rafael** (AEL-0261).
- **Mara's loss** (AEL-0388, 9 October 2031) was paid for by a man's fall from scaffolding at Martim Moniz, at 07:15 Lisbon time.

From the first weeks, Lua dreams of **a red door in a whitewashed wall, a valley of terraced cane fields, a chapel bell at noon, and a woman singing a morna.** She draws them obsessively.

### 4.4 The Tagus Miracle (18 June 2032, 21:14)

In a sudden squall, the commuter ferry *Barreiro Nova* collides with a container ship's tender and capsizes mid-river. 212 people are in the water, and 41 are dying. Lua is on the riverbank. The Heft is the heaviest she has ever felt. She says yes.

The ferry is upright, undamaged, its passengers dry on deck, remembering the water. The footage is seen around the world. The next morning's headline: **AMNESTY.**

**That same minute,** 3,400 km away, the ~900 residents of **Ribeira da Prata**, a valley village on Santo Antão in Cape Verde, **forget their shared history.** They keep their private memories but lose everything they hold in common: why the bell rings at noon on 3 May, the words of their songs, the names of the relatives who emigrated, the dead they mourned together. *They still know their children. They no longer know why they're a village.* It is also, unknown to anyone, **Ivone's birthplace.**

### 4.5 The confrontation (Feb 2033)

Following her dreams, Lua finds the valley: the red door, the terraces, the chapel. She believes she has found home. The villagers, emptied, take her in: *"Maybe you were one of us. We don't remember."* She becomes close to **Djina Lopes** (§6.5), the schoolteacher.

**11 February 2033:** Mara Okafor publishes the **Okafor Correlation**. The register holds 1,140 people: 228 individual cases plus the 912 villagers of case group 0632. They fall in 229 onset events, and **every one of the 31 publicly known Amnesty rescues matches an onset to the minute**. The other 198 events match nothing anyone reported, which tells the world she has been saving far more people than it knew. Case 0632 is Ribeira da Prata, at 21:14 on 18 June 2032.

Lua reads it at the village fountain. That same evening, Djina shows her the house with the red door, her mother's house. Lua recognises the song Djina's mother sang **because she dreamed it.** It was never Lua's own past; it was Djina's. → **Revelation 1.**

## 5. Conflict

### 5.1 The central dilemma

**How much of humanity can one person sacrifice in the name of saving it?**

Each save is a clear good: a living person, now. Each cost is invisible, delayed and spread out, and it adds up. The arithmetic always favours saving, because a life outweighs a memory. But a humanity that is alive and has forgotten itself might no longer be humanity. After Revelation 2 the dilemma gets sharper and becomes personal: **she isn't only deciding *whether* someone pays, but *who*.**

### 5.2 How she misunderstands her power

| BELIEF | FACT |
|---|---|
| "I heal." | She moves forgetting from the world into people. |
| "The cost is random." | It falls on what she holds closest (L4). |
| "My dreams are my past." | They're payers' memories. |
| "It started on a night I can't remember." | It started five months earlier, and she **chose** to forget. |
| "The people I love are safe from it." | They're the most at risk. |

### 5.3 The opposing force: **Dr Ingrid Solvang and the Protocol**

Solvang (§6.3) offers the **Protocol for Consensual Allocation**, known as **"the Tithe."** Saves are triaged, and costs are steered onto **consenting, paid volunteers**: Lua is shown the volunteer's photograph and holds that face in mind as she says yes.

Solvang **knows L4 before Lua does.** She proved it on herself. That is why she is a real threat: she understands Lua's power better than Lua does.

**Background texture, not antagonists:** **the Archivists**, a civilian movement that writes everything down on paper, and **the Unsaved**, who wear *DO NOT SAVE* tags.

### 5.4 Why defeating Solvang solves nothing

- Every save still costs someone. Stopping Solvang doesn't stop the Ledger.
- The Protocol may be **the most ethical option available**, because it replaces unknowing strangers with consenting ones. Defeating it means going back to Lua's longing deciding who forgets.
- Once a market exists, the poor sell first. Removing Solvang doesn't remove the market, and another government would build its own Office.
- The only ways to end the cost are to **stop saving** or to **pay it herself.** Neither is a victory.

### 5.5 Open mysteries (intentional)

| Mystery | Status |
|---|---|
| **What *is* the Ledger:** a natural law, a mind, or her? | **Open.** Never fully answered. |
| **Why Celeste?** One seed: Ivone's husband Abílio survived the 1970 wreck of the schooner *Flor de Maio* "by a miracle," then couldn't remember his own wedding. There may have been others. The Continuity Office finds the harbour log in October 2033 (archive record LETHE-HIS-1970): 2 March 1970, 23:40, Mindelo, eight crew lost, one survivor aged 32, a watchman who heard "nothing" and saw a lamp "with no colour in it". Who undid the wreck, and who paid besides Abílio, stays unanswered. | **Open.** Reserved for future stories. |
| **Do the memories exist anywhere after they're taken?** She dreams some of them. Whether all of them go somewhere is unknown. They can never be returned (L3). | **Open.** |

**Not mysteries, but FACT (don't turn them into puzzles):**
- The **seven-minute** window has no explanation in the story.
- **Why her likeness can't be held:** a face is remembered by attaching it to *someone*, and she has no identity for it to attach to. **As she accepts the name Celeste, her likeness begins to hold**, first for Ivone, then for others. The cost of knowing who she is: becoming recognisable, and trackable.

## 6. Supporting characters

### 6.1 The ally who believes: **Duarte Mbala**

- 61, born in Luanda, Lisbon since childhood. Blind since 24. He was for twenty years the voice of a late-night radio show, *Rádio Insónia*, and now repairs radios.
- **He recognises Lua by voice.** That isn't a mystical sense; he has spent a working life listening to voices. To him she is not a blur. She is *a Voz*, "the Voice."
- He listens to **emergency scanners** all night and starts sending her to incidents, which breaks her "only in front of me" rule.
- **What he believes:** lives come before memories, and she is "the best thing that has ever happened to this species." **His flaw:** loneliness, and a hunger for meaning that he has attached to her importance.

### 6.2 The one who distrusts her: **Dr Mara Okafor**

- 44, British-Nigerian neurologist and epidemiologist, seconded from the WHO to Lisbon. She named **Acute Episodic Loss (AEL)** and published the Okafor Correlation.
- **She is a payer.** In October 2031 she lost every memory of her son **Tobi**'s first five years. She knows he is hers and loves him, but she cannot remember him small. Tobi is eleven, and he keeps showing her photographs she can't feel anything about.
- **Why she distrusts Lua:** as a scientist, because an uncontrolled person is running a global experiment on human memory; and personally, because she was part of it.
- **Her arc:** from hunting Lua, to an uneasy alliance, to being the one person who tells Lua the truth without worship or hatred. She is **the visitor's guide through the evidence** on the website.

### 6.3 The antagonist: **Dr Ingrid Solvang**

- 58, Norwegian bioethicist. She chaired a WHO committee on ICU triage ethics, so she has made allocation decisions about lives before. She is Director of the **Continuity Office** (*Gabinete de Continuidade*).
- **Her motive:** *"A miracle without a protocol is a lottery."* She believes improvised mercy causes more harm than structure.
- **Her advantage:** in 2033 she ran an experiment on Lua without her knowledge. She staged an emergency where Lua would see it and placed **her own photograph** in Lua's line of sight. Solvang forgot her husband. She now carries his name on an index card. She **knows L4 is true**, and she has paid to know it.
- **Her line:** *"You decide who forgets every single day, Lua. I'm only asking you to decide fairly."*
- **She challenges Lua's worldview, not her body.** She never fights her. She offers her a better system and makes refusing it look selfish.

### 6.4 From her past: **Ivone Semedo**

- 91 in 2033. Grandmother. In the 1960s she sang mornas in the cafés of Mindelo. She was born in **Ribeira da Prata**.
- She has advanced dementia with **lucid hours**. In them, she remembers Celeste and asks for her. Nobody believes her claim that her granddaughter "went into the light."
- She can't recognise Lua by sight, until the day Lua hums a morna Ivone taught Celeste when she was six. Ivone stops: *"Celeste. Onde é que tu estiveste?"* ("Celeste. Where have you been?")
- **Two clocks:** her lucid hours are getting fewer, because the disease was never undone. And once Lua lets herself love Ivone, **Ivone is in the cost's path** (L4).

### 6.5 The face of the payers: **Djina Lopes**

- 36, primary-school teacher in Ribeira da Prata. **Her mother is the singing woman in Lua's dreams.** Djina has forgotten her mother's songs, and her village's.
- She is Lua's first real friend after the Correlation, and later she knows exactly what Lua did.
- **The only grace the story allows:** Lua can't give Djina back her memories, but she **can teach her the songs again**, from the dreams. Djina learns them **as new**. This is the series' single image of repair, and it never breaks L3.

## 7. World

### 7.1 Setting

- **2030–2034, the near future.** Everything is photographed, logged and backed up, which makes the forgetting more horrifying. *The records survive; the meaning doesn't.* People look at their wedding photos and feel nothing.
- **Lisbon:** a port of departures and saudade, built on the memory of the 1755 earthquake. Main locations are Mouraria (Duarte's flat), Cais do Sodré and the Tagus, Casa Santa Luzia, and the Continuity Office (in a former customs house on the river).
- **Ribeira da Prata** (fictional), Santo Antão, Cape Verde.
- **Global reach:** payers on every continent.

### 7.2 Reactions

| Who | Reaction |
|---|---|
| **Governments** | Panic, then appetite. The EU creates the **Continuity Office** to run *Case LETHE*. Other states want access of their own. Courts debate whether someone who forgot their marriage is still bound by it. |
| **Scientists** | **WHO AEL Surveillance** (Mara's unit). Payers' brain scans show **nothing**: the memory leaves no trace. That is the most frightening finding. |
| **Media** | First a saint, then **A Ladra**, the thief. Images centre on the ghosted smear. Columnists point out that *amnesty* and *amnesia* share a root. |
| **Ordinary people** | Shrines at the river. **The Archivists**: paper ledgers, letters to their future selves, name tattoos. **The Unsaved**: *DO NOT SAVE* tags. A question asked in schools: *would you rather die, or have a stranger forget their mother?* |

### 7.3 What it does to society

- Every rescue turns into a moral argument after the fact.
- **Memory gets a price** once the Tithe exists, and the poor sell first.
- People wake up as strangers to their spouses. Marriages, inheritances and legal identities are left in limbo.
- **Communities that paid collectively are living ruins: the people alive, the history gone.**

### 8.2 Three revelations

1. **"The Borrowed Past"** (Feb 2033). Her dreams aren't her memories. They're the payers'. The singing woman is Djina's mother. The self Lua has been rebuilding is made from what she took.
2. **"It Was Never Random"** (late 2033). Solvang tells her, or Mara works it out from the data: the cost falls on what Lua holds closest. Her longing has been steering it all along, toward echoes of her own loss, toward the diaspora, toward Ribeira da Prata, **her grandmother's birthplace**. And now that she knows, **every future cost is a decision.** The people she has started to love (Duarte, Mara, Tobi, Ivone, Djina) are now in the path.
3. **"Night Zero Wasn't the Beginning"** (early 2034). Mara obtains the missing-person file. In the evidence box is Celeste's phone. In the Night Archive, **in her own voice**, the only part of her that survived intact, is the confession: five months of undoings, a dozen dementia patients who paid, and the decision to erase herself. **Lua was a hero first, a thief second, and her own victim third, and she chose the order.** She now has to decide whether to become Celeste again.

### 8.3 The betrayal

**Duarte** gives Solvang Lua's **voiceprint** (the one thing about her that can be tracked), her routines, and his scanner logs. Solvang has shown him the proof of L4, so he knows two things: that Lua could steer costs onto volunteers and save thousands, and that he is now in the path himself. He tells himself it's for her good and the world's. It's her own logic turned against her: *the greater good justifies the cost.*

### 8.4 The devastating choice: **"The Lisbon Choice"**

A major earthquake hits Lisbon, echoing 1755, and a surge follows up the Tagus. Tens of thousands will die within the window. The Heft weighs as much as a people's entire shared history. Solvang's volunteers are on hand with their photographs, and **they aren't enough.** Lua has three options, and each one costs her:

1. **Let her longing choose,** as it always has. The Cape Verdean diaspora forgets its shared past. The mornas disappear from human memory, including the ones she taught Djina.
2. **Hold herself.** She pays with everything she has become since Night Zero: Duarte's voice, Mara's truth, Djina's songs, Ivone's *"Celeste."* She becomes *Desconhecida* again, permanently.
3. **Refuse.** The city dies, and she remembers every person.

**The outcome is not decided in the bible.** The website's final chapter puts the choice to the visitor.

### 8.5 Future directions

1. **The Precedent:** the 1970 *Flor de Maio* wreck, and earlier forgetting epidemics in the archives. Was there someone before her? Is there someone now?
2. **The Market:** after the Tithe, brokers, coerced donors, and rich people buying saves.
3. **The Saved and the Paid:** an anthology, each story pairing one survivor with the person who paid for them.
4. **Tobi:** Mara's son trying to make his mother remember him being small. A domestic story inside a global one.
5. **Successor:** what happens to the Ledger if Lua dies? Solvang wants to find out.
6. **Ribeira da Prata Learns Again:** a village building a new history from songs taught by the woman who erased the old one.

## 9. Visual identity

### 9.1 Silhouette (readable in solid black)

- **Long, heavy, knee-length knit cardigan in grey-blue** (from the Santa Luzia uniform, worn soft). In silhouette it reads as a long tapered column.
- **Over-ear radio headphones worn around her neck**, Duarte's old studio pair, cabled to a scanner in her pocket. They form a distinctive loop at the collar line.
- **Cropped head**, her hair kept short since hospital (short natural coils, about 3 cm). The head shape reads clearly.
- **Right-handed**, which is why the inscription is on her **left** hand.
- **The locked reference images** are in [concept/reference/](concept/reference/README.md). Every depiction derives from them.
- **The carer's kneel:** her signature pose. One knee down, one hand flat on the injured person or the ground, head bowed. She never stands over people. **AMNESTY is recognised by her kneeling.**
- Dark work trousers and non-slip care shoes. Height 1.68 m, lean, leaning in, steady hands.

### 9.2 The emblem: **the blank badge**

The Santa Luzia lanyard badge, with its name card missing: **a blank white rectangle** on her chest. *(FACT: on Night Zero, Ivone's hand tore the name card away, and she has kept it ever since. She returns it after Revelation 3. Full arc in [VISUAL_DIRECTION.md](VISUAL_DIRECTION.md) §6.)* It's the series' single emblem: an identity with nothing written on it. It doubles as the visual system's main shape (redaction bars, white-out, empty fields) for covers, logo and interface.

### 9.3 How to show her face (required for all artists)

- **In drawn panels:** her face is **hidden by staging, never by erasure.** Use cropping, a turned head, back-lighting, shadow, a reflection that breaks, or foreground objects. Her skin, hands, neck and posture are fully and accurately drawn as a Black Cape Verdean woman.
- **In in-world photos and video:** a **ghosted motion smear**, like a long exposure, with **her colours kept**. It is never a white or blank shape.
- **Never:** a white blob, white-out, bleach or overexposure over her face. **White is for the world's forgetting, not for her.**
- **From Revelation 3 on,** as her likeness begins to hold, her face can start to appear, partially and at moments of chosen identity. That's a planned visual turning point.

### 9.4 Colour, sound and motion

- **The world is in full, warm colour:** Lisbon light, Cape Verdean blues and ochres.
- **Lua's palette is quiet:** grey-blue, bone, ink.
- **Undoing:** colour drains outward from her to paper-grey, **while she alone keeps full colour**. Sound cuts, the harm reverses, and colour floods back from the edges (~2 s).
- **The headphones are removed after Duarte's betrayal.** This is the one planned change to her silhouette (VISUAL_DIRECTION §6).
- **The blink** (on the payer's side): a single white frame and silence. *(The payer lives it as "half a second of white silence" (§3.4); an observer, or the page, sees a single frame. Both are canon.)*
- **Silence is her sound.** Every undoing cuts all audio.

## 11. Summary

### Character summary

**Lua**, called **AMNESTY**, is in truth **Celeste Semedo**, a former dementia carer from the Cape Verdean diaspora who can make physical harm unhappen. Every undoing costs someone memory of equal weight. The cost falls on whatever she holds closest in the instant she says yes. She has no past, no papers, and a face no one can hold. She saves people because it's the only time she feels like someone. She believes the cost is random. It has been steered by her own longing toward her own people, it will come for everyone she loves, and it began months before the night she chose to forget it.

### Three defining characteristics

1. **She has no past.** She is built entirely from what she does.
2. **Her mercy has a price.** Every good she does is paid for by someone else's memory, and paid for by whatever she holds dear.
3. **She cannot be held.** She is the most important person in the world, and no one can hold her in mind.

### The central mystery

**Who pays for each miracle, and why them?** The story's answer: whatever Lua holds closest. That has been her longing, then her people, and soon the people she loves. Behind it sits the question that is never fully answered: what the Ledger *is*.

### The emotional promise

*You will come to love someone who is erasing the world, and understand why she can't stop.* The story's feeling is **saudade**: grief for something you can't remember, and the question of whether being alive is worth more than being remembered.
